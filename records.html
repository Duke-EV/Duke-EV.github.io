Record file · Eta
Guinness World Record · Most Efficient Electric Vehicle

Eta

Named for the Greek letter engineers use to denote efficiency, Eta was designed with a singular goal: to hold the title of "World's Most Efficient Electric Vehicle."

The vehicle features significantly improved aerodynamics compared to Maxwell, a highly-efficient direct drive motor, a tracking step-down converter to minimize motor controller switching losses, and a numerically optimized driver strategy.

On record day, driving at an average of 15.0 mph with a 50 kg driver, the same conditions as the Shell Eco-marathon, Eta posted 797 mi/kWh, breaking TU Munich's record of 765.

At 27,482 MPGe, Eta could drive around the world on less than a gallon of gasoline equivalent.

Record file · Maxwell
Guinness World Record · Most Fuel-Efficient Vehicle

Maxwell

On July 21, 2018, our hydrogen-powered Maxwell broke the PAC-Car II's long-standing world record for fuel efficiency, setting the new bar at 14,573 MPG, enough to circumnavigate the globe on the energy in a single gallon of gasoline.

Maxwell uses a dead-end anode fuel cell with passive humidification, which allows for simple operation.

Every loss in the vehicle, from aerodynamic drag to bearing drag to tire rolling resistance, was individually quantified to build a unified model of the car's performance.

The road to the records

A decade of iteration

The early years (2011-2016)

First cars, first lessons

From a heavy first build with an in-hub brush motor to aluminum frames, custom bodies, and our first custom motor controller.

2017

First place, Shell Eco-marathon Americas

Maxwell wins Battery-Electric Prototype at 296 mi/kWh with a carbon fiber monocoque and custom electronics.

2018

World Record #1, and a competition sweep

Maxwell repeats first place (368 mi/kWh), our backup car Gemini wins the hydrogen prototype class, and on July 21, hydrogen-powered Maxwell sets the Guinness World Record for fuel efficiency: 14,573 MPG.

2019

World Record #2

Eta sets the Guinness World Record for the most efficient electric vehicle: 797 mi/kWh, a record that still stands.
